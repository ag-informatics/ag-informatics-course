Essential Difficulties of Software

  • Complexity
  • No two systems are alike. Software has many possible states. Nonlinear increase in complexity. Math is hard. Management is difficult.
  • How to represent the complex interplay between agricultural, social, environmental, and economic aspects of a farm?
  • Conformity
  • No unifying theories about goodness, correctness, usefulness.
  • How to decide what elements of a farm to focus on in software?
  • Changeability
  • Software needs to be adapted. One change = dependencies galore. Maintenance is hard. Technology evolves. Humans change.
  • Food and ag systems are dynamic and temporally complex. How to manage?
  • Invisibility
  • If a line of code fails in a computer and you don't get an error message, did anything break?
  • Balance between visibility of farms and invisibility of, for instance, food flows.

  • Text, images.

    • No: Nominal. e.g., trees
    • Yes: Ordinal. e.g., rating

  • Quantitative

  • Numerical.
  • Fixed increment? Discrete. Otherwise, Continuous.
  • Does 0 matter?
    • No: Interval. e.g., date/time
    • Yes: Ratio. e.g., weight

The Data Lifecycle - Understand

Mapping the problem space

  • What is the problem/question?

  • What is the goal?

  • Who are the actors? What are their needs?

  • What data are required?

The Data Lifecycle - Plan

Mapping the solution space

  • How will data be collected/acquired?

  • How will you process and analyze it?

  • How will the data be used and/or shared?

  • How will you handle documentation, data management, quality control?

The Data Lifecycle - Acquire

Getting the data you need

  • BYO data: collect it from the world - interviews, sensors, samples.

  • Private/protected data: a sharing agreement with the data owner.

  • Public data: available on the internet, with terms attached.

  • Whose data is it, and what are you permitted to do with it?

The Data Lifecycle - Process

Making the data usable

  • Clean data: identify missing values and duplicates. Consider obvious errors e.g., negative numbers, allowed ranges.

  • Check data types: is that number stored as text? Does that date parse?

  • Structure: one observation per row, one variable per column.

  • Track changes: archive raw data, archive data at each processing step.

The Data Lifecycle - Analyze

Converting data to information

  • Describe the data: size, shape, distributions, outliers.

  • Visualize the data: visualize simple trends, patterns, and gaps in the data.

  • Data analysis: begin with exploratory data analysis, then answer your data science question.

  • Document your process: maintain a "lab" notebook to track what you did, why you did it, and what you learned.

The Data Lifecycle - Share / Use

Informing action

  • Define use: Who are the data users and data stakeholders? What is the intended use of the data?

  • Design for sharing: How can you best support user-data interactions? Graphs, dashboards, interactive web apps?

  • Data metadata: provenance, scope, limits, assumptions, bias in the data.

  • Data protocols: Share the method AND the data, not just the results.

The Data Lifecycle - Meta

Meta data work is perennial

  • Documentation: explains and tracks data provenance, workflow, and your manipulations.

  • Quality control: a continuous improvement process, from initial manual "checks" to more formalized data verification and validation.

  • Data management: naming and versioning conventions, storage and backup protocols, access and data sharing.

  • For good data habits early = useful preliminary results!
