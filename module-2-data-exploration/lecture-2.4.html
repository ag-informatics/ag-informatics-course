Produce a line chart of Indiana's average monthly temperature from January 1970 through December 2021 — one data point per month, time running along the x-axis.

  • 1. Filter raw data

  • R1 In plain English, describe the filtering logic you need

  • 2. Plot the data

  • R3 Sketch a mock-up of your chart

A DataFrame df holding Average Monthly Temperature by US State. Each row is one state in one month of one year; the data covers 1950–2021.

Full solution and grading rubric

Filter raw data R1 + R2

R1 - plain English (the pseudocode)

Keep only the rows where state equals "Indiana" and year is greater than or equal to 1970 — that is, 1970 through 2021, all 12 months of each year.

From those rows I will plot average_temp on the y-axis against time on the x-axis, where time comes from combining the year and month columns.

Plot the data R3 + R4

R3 - grade on the presence of components

  • Line chart form — a continuous line, not bars/scatter/pie
  • Descriptive title (e.g. "Indiana Average Monthly Temperature, 1970–2021")
  • X-axis has a label naming time (Year / Date / Month)
  • X-axis shows plausible tick values (e.g. 1970, 1980, … 2020)
  • Y-axis has a label naming temperature
  • Y-axis label or ticks show units (°F) and a plausible range (roughly 15–80)

Q: Indiana temp variance across year?

average_temp: one number. month: one ordinal category. 52 Januaries, 52 Februaries...: several observations per group.

Question: Which graph is better?

Violin shows shape: Jan-Mar have long, thin lower tails, i.e., few very cold months. Box plot shows as a few outlier circles.

Data: Justin Wong, Average Monthly Temperature by US State, Kaggle, from NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance. Plotted 1970-2021.

Evaluate our Quiz Data + Viz

Truthful? Functional? Beautiful? Insightful? Enlightening?

  • 52 years × 12 months: mostly the seasons
  • The data contains: monthly_mean_from_1901_to_2000
  • Question: Is Indiana hotter?
  • Answer: Unclear! The seasons mask it.

Data: Justin Wong, Average Monthly Temperature by US State, Kaggle, from NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance. Plotted 1970-2021.

Is Indiana hotter?

Yes. Months averaged 0.5 °F below their 1901-2000 mean in the 1970s, and 1.35 °F above it in the 2010s.
But: Visualization is correct for this file. Is the data + data source correct?

Data: Justin Wong, Average Monthly Temperature by US State, Kaggle, from NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance. Plotted 1970-2021.

A clue that something's off:
the 1901-2000 means

Each month has a "1901-2000 mean": that month's average over the whole 20th century. It should be one fixed number per month.

A mean over 1901-2000 is one number per month. Why two?

Something in how the file was made. Go to the source.

Where did this data come from?

Clues about accuracy and provenance:
Kaggle data is a copy. Cites NOAA's Climate at a Glance, with no license.

Screenshot: Justin Wong, Average Monthly Temperature by US State, Kaggle, 2026-09-16.

What travelled with it?

The file says "monthly". Nothing says how the months were computed.

Screenshot: Justin Wong, Average Monthly Temperature by US State, Kaggle, 2026-09-16.

How far did the copy travel?

1,323 downloads. Every one inherits whatever is missing.

Screenshot: Justin Wong, Average Monthly Temperature by US State, Kaggle, 2026-09-16.

NOAA data source

NOAA adjusts and revises: expect small differences from a 2022 copy and consider how you process/visualize.

Climate at a Glance: Data Outputs (video), NOAA NCEI; Climate at a Glance, NOAA NCEI, 2026-09-16.

Kaggle data citation?

NOAA's citation names the page and the date, not any settings. Kaggle's reference is correct, BUT the URL returns a one-month map, not 72 years of months.

Climate at a Glance: Data Outputs (video), NOAA NCEI; Climate at a Glance: Regional Mapping, NOAA NCEI, 2026-09-16.

Data for a timeseries?

Time Scale is a setting: 1-month, 2-month, year-to-date... It's in the form and in the API path. It's nowhere in the Kaggle file.

Climate at a Glance: Data Outputs (video), NOAA NCEI; Climate at a Glance: Statewide Time Series, NOAA NCEI, 2026-09-16.

What's in the two datasets?

Kaggle's "December" is NOAA's 2-month November-December average: within 0.1 °F in all 72 years, and every other month matches its 2-month series too. Against true December, it's off by up to 12.5 °F.

Kaggle's December 1901-2000 mean: 35.6-35.7 °F. NOAA's: 30.3 °F.

Data: NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance: Statewide Time Series, Indiana, Average Temperature, 1-month (December) and 2-month (November-December), 1950-2021, and Statewide Mapping, December 2022, retrieved 2026-09-16; Justin Wong, Kaggle.

Implications

Still true

  • Indiana is warming
  • Winter varies more than summer
  • The histogram's humps are seasons
  • 2021's warmest states, and the latitude pattern

Not quite

  • "Monthly": each month is that month plus the one before
  • "January" includes December
  • The "1901-2000 means" are 2-month normals
  • The 0.1 °F diff: likely rounding error

The charts were correct for the data file. The data file wasn't "correct".

The right tool: NOAA's Haywood plot

Is Indiana hotter? Three of the five warmest years are since 2012. The source's own chart, with its settings on the page.

NOAA NCEI, Climate at a Glance: Statewide Haywood (Indiana, Average Temperature, January-December), retrieved 2026-09-16, https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/climate-at-a-glance/statewide/haywood

AI-assisted debugging

Human evaluation. AI data crunching. Data source verification.

Step Who
0 Data exploration: Producing a TON of simple charts all on the same data. Instructor prompting based on course materials.
1 Noticed: the "1901-2000 mean" should be one number per month. It had two (January: 28.5 and 28.4). First guess: rounding. AI reports results.
2 Verified: downloaded NOAA's own data. The download settings were not recorded on Kaggle. Instructor
3 Compared: December normal is 35.7 °F in Kaggle, 30.3 °F at NOAA. Too big for rounding. AI report, Instructor assess
4 Hypothesis: each Kaggle "month" is a 2-month average. AI propose, Instructor assess
5 Tested against NOAA: 2-month matches within 0.1 °F every year; 1-month is off by up to 14.5 °F. AI reports to Instructor
6 Checked more: Alabama and Wyoming match too. AI reports to Instructor
7 Review, verify, think, share! Instructor, with AI-assist

Practicing Trust with Data

Privacy

  • Protect personal information

  • Handle geolocation information with sensitivity

  • Provide granular data access controls

Agency

  • Come to early consensus on data ownership

  • Provide situational control over data

  • Empower stakeholder voices


Raturi et al., Forthcoming (2021), "Cultivating Trust in Technology-Mediated Sustainable Agricultural Research".

Cultivate Trust in your Process

Transparency

  • Be inclusive of stakeholders in the process

  • Provide feedback and clarity on the process

  • Open source development of tools

  • Open access to privacy-preserved data

Accountability

  • Uphold the responsibility of protecting data and research stakeholder rights

  • Provide traceability of action

  • Provide feedback among stakeholders

Raturi et al., Forthcoming (2021), "Cultivating Trust in Technology-Mediated Sustainable Agricultural Research".

Sensitivity of Data

  • Private: Some Farmer Data, Proprietary Data, Human Subjects Research Data

  • Protected: Same as above, but can be released in certain ways (e.g., de-identified, aggregated, shared with certain people)

  • Public: Publicly funded research data, "Generic" data e.g., general plant data, "utility" data e.g., weather, can also include protected data e.g., deidentified data.
