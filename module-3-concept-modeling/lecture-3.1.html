What is modeling?

Modeling is the process of abstracting a system in the real world, such that only the attributes pertinent to a particular analysis remain.

Physical Models: Soil conservation

Robert Eikleberry, "Block Diagrams for Soil Survey Interpretations", USDA Soil Conservation Service report.

Biophysical Models: Bioinformatics

1. Observation: "high-quality data through experiments [to] identify patterns, propose hypotheses, [... understand] the biological phenomenon"

2. Simulation: "computational tools [... to] translate our observations into detailed simulations"

3. Reduced Order Modeling: "models that encapsulate the core mechanisms of the systems under investigation."

4. Analysis: "analyze both simulations and models to uncover new insights, identify critical parameters, and validate results against experiments"

Biophysical Modeling, Center for Computational Biology, Flatiron Institute, Simons Foundation.

Biophysical Models: Crop systems

Simulate biophysical processes in a system. Mathematical equations and parameters derived from empirical studies.
Bottom-up design of a specialized dynamic system model. A physical and mathematical model, with encoded logic.

DSSAT model components, DSSAT Foundation.

Concept Models: Crop ontology

Represent system components and the relationships among them. Provide "rules" of operation in a domain of interest and a baseline model.
Iterative design of a generalized conceptual model. A logical model, that supports physical and mathematical modeling.

Crop Ontology, CGIAR.

What gets generalized?

Modeling requirements

  • Need standardized data structures across heterogenous data types.
  • Need a a consistent modeling workflow spanning across devices.
  • Need to support varying levels of abstraction, formality, and granularity.
  • Need to brdige the data reusability gap.
  • Need to support change tracking in dynamic farms.
  • Need to bridge original data collection reasons and actual data use cases.
  • Need for increased support for coordination and collaboration on farms.

What gets generalized?

Data scopes

  1. A point in space & time.
  2. The system as a whole.
  3. A subcomponent.
  4. System behavior.
  5. System in the future.
  6. Inputs to the system.
