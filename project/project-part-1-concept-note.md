# Agricultural Informatics Project - The Big Picture

In Project Part 1, you will "pitch" a concept regarding an informatics problem that people encounter in agriculture, food systems, ecology, and related areas of research and practice. 

In Project Part 2, you will ideate and describe 5 possible solutions, whether they are entirely separate alternatives, variations on a theme, or components of a larger solution. You will explore these solutions to consider both technical feasibility and user-centered design implications.

In Project Part 3, you will write a software engineering specification document that puts together your problem statement (building on project part 1), your proposed solution (synthesizing from project part 2). You will articulate your users' requirements, software design and architecture, and your intended implementation strategy. 

By the end of this project, you will have described a proposed solution for a domain-specific problem. You can envision the final deliverable as a project description for a hackathon, as a product idea around which to build a business plan, or as first-draft of a graduate research proposal to discuss with your advisor! 

# Project Part 1: Concept Note

**Goal**: Identify a domain-specific problem that may require the development of new or improved digital tools.

**Required**: Write a 1-page, single-spaced concept note describing your problem of interest. You must include **3** peer-reviewed article refrences to justify or rationalize your concept. As you describe your issue, make sure you answer these questions:

  - What is the problem/question? Why is this an informatics problem?
  - What is your project goal? Does this fit into your research or other work?
  - Who is affected by this problem? Who are the actors? What are their needs?
  - Why has this problem not been solved yet? What data are required?
  - What might "appropriate" solutions look like?

This assignment focuses on **mapping the problem space** applies the same approach used in Module 2 when framing a data problem, while integrating the conceptual modeling workflow from Module 3. It is critical to understand the problem correctly to create an appropriate solution, so this is where we are starting.

Check the rubric to see what you should provide in your concept note!

**What this assignment is not:** In this assignment, you should not describe how to solve the problem, or the work that would need to be done to solve it. Tell me what **appropriate solutions** look like at a high-level only, but don't start writing out how you'd build it. Ask if you're uncertain!

**Page limits and writing style**: Write colloquially: think concept pitch email. The page limit is not a hard limit. It is a suggestion to show how much description you will need to get full credit if you write clearly. It is perfectly possible that someone could completely fulfill the requirements in as little as 3/4 of a page, while other writers or topics may require closer to 1.5 pages. You should not go under or over those limits, as those are indications that you are either including unnecessary information or not justifying your reasons.

**Regarding originality**: Your paper cannot just be a rewrite of an article you found online. You cannot just rehash a discussion point that people have been writing about in the exact same way for years. For example, there are many discussions on "smart" irrigation systems and why using IoT sensors in the field is the way to attack the problem and deliver many economic, social and environmental benefits. Picking a generic problem like this will result in a low originality grade, as it has already been done. That said, many problems in agriculture are centuries old. You do not need to come up with an entirely new problem, but the approach to the problem should be yours, and not immediately identifiable as already widely discussed.

## AI Use Policy

ALL parts of this project involve:

> **No AI-generated content.** Do not use AI tools to draft, rewrite, or summarize your concept note. **Writing is thinking**, and the act of writing out your thoughts from scratch will help you refine your ideas, as well as support our ability to evaluate your concept. Discussion and ideation with AI is fine per the general Academic Integrity policy, as is using it to find and understand literature. But remember: YOU are responsible for the content, you must verify what you learn using ALL digital tools, whether search engines or LLMs. YOU are responsible for your concept, and if you can't verbally explain what you've written, then we will have to discuss how you produced your content.
